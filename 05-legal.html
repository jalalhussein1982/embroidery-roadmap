Czech Tax and Legal Caveats

A sole maker in Prague selling made-to-order and ready-made goods via Instagram, DM and a link page, to consumers in CZ, the EU and occasionally beyond.

Orientation, not advice

Figures marked verify change yearly or depend on Anna’s personal situation — employment status, parental leave. Confirm them with a Czech accountant (účetní) before relying on them. A one-hour consultation costs roughly 1,500–2,500 Kč and is the cheapest insurance in this whole document.

The sequenceOrder of operations

Do these in order. Step 7 is the one that unlocks file 02.

Section 1The trap that matters most — selling without a trade licence

The rule

Regular, independent activity for profit is podnikání. It requires a trade licence (živnostenské oprávnění) under the Trade Licensing Act, zákon č. 455/1991 Sb. Thirty-five sales posts over four months with a DM order funnel is systematic activity, not a hobby. Trading without a licence is neoprávněné podnikání — an administrative offence, and above a threshold a criminal one (§251 trestního zákoníku).

The hobby exception does not apply

Occasional income under §10 odst. 3 písm. a) of the Income Tax Act is tax-exempt up to 50,000 Kč/year verify — raised from 30,000 in 2024, but only for genuinely occasional activity. A public page soliciting orders is not occasional, regardless of turnover.

Section 3VAT — irrelevant now, one hidden trap later

The trap · identifikovaná osoba

If a Czech non-VAT trader buys a service from a business in another country — Meta advertising (Ireland), Canva Pro (Australia), Linktree Pro, Shopify, Etsy fees — she must register as an identified person (§6h zákona o DPH) within 15 days, declare and pay Czech VAT on that service under reverse charge at 21%, and file a VAT return for that month.

It does not make her a full VAT payer and does not add VAT to her sales, but failing to register is a fineable omission that catches many small sellers — one more reason the “no paid Meta ads” decision is convenient.

Apify is a Czech company (Apify Technologies s.r.o., Prague), so Apify subscriptions do not trigger this.

Section 4Consumer law — made-to-order goods

Sources: občanský zákoník (č. 89/2012 Sb.) and zákon o ochraně spotřebitele (č. 634/1992 Sb.). A DM order is a distance contract and consumer rules apply in full.

PointRuleAction
Right of withdrawal The 14-day withdrawal (§1829 OZ) does not apply to goods made to the consumer’s specification or personalised (§1837 písm. d). It does apply to the ready-made “skladem / in stock” pieces sold remotely. State clearly before confirming an order that custom pieces cannot be returned; accept 14-day returns on stock pieces.
Pre-contract information Seller identity, IČO, address, total price, delivery cost, lead time, complaint procedure, note on withdrawal (§1820 OZ). Put these on the linked page as Obchodní podmínky / Terms. A DM confirming “price X, delivery Y, custom = no return” satisfies the contract-level duty.
Price display Prices offered to consumers must be final, with delivery stated separately (§13 zák. 634/1992) verify section “od 890 Kč” in the bio is acceptable as indicative. The confirmed DM price must be final.
Defects / warranty The consumer may claim defects appearing within 2 years; complaints must be resolved within 30 days (§2165, §2172–2173 OZ) verify post-2023 numbering Handmade does not exempt her. Photograph every piece before handover.
Dispute resolution Consumers must be told that the Czech Trade Inspection (ČOI) is the ADR body (§14 zák. 634/1992). One line in the terms.
Deposits A deposit (záloha) for custom work is lawful and advisable — 30–50%. State it in the order steps.
