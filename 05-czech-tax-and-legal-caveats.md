# 05 · Czech Tax and Legal Caveats

Scope: a sole maker in Prague selling made-to-order and ready-made goods via Instagram, DM and (later) a link page, to consumers in CZ, the EU and occasionally beyond.

This is orientation, not advice. Figures marked **[verify]** change yearly or depend on Anna's personal situation (employment status, parental leave). Confirm them with a Czech accountant (účetní) before relying on them — a one-hour consultation costs ~1,500–2,500 Kč and is the cheapest insurance here.

---

## 1. The trap that matters most: selling without a trade licence

**Rule.** Regular, independent activity for profit is *podnikání*. It requires a trade licence (živnostenské oprávnění) under the Trade Licensing Act (zákon č. 455/1991 Sb.). Thirty-five sales posts over four months with a DM order funnel is systematic activity, not a hobby. Trading without a licence is *neoprávněné podnikání* — an administrative offence, and above a threshold a criminal one (§251 trestního zákoníku).

**The hobby exception does not apply.** Occasional income under §10 odst. 3 písm. a) of the Income Tax Act is tax-exempt up to **50,000 Kč/year [verify — raised from 30,000 in 2024]**, but only for genuinely *occasional* activity. A public page soliciting orders is not occasional, regardless of turnover.

**What to do.**
- Register a **volná živnost** (free trade): "Výroba, obchod a služby neuvedené v přílohách 1 až 3 živnostenského zákona", field "Výroba textilií, textilních výrobků, oděvů a oděvních doplňků" **[verify field number]**. No qualification required.
- File at any živnostenský úřad or online via the Jednotný registrační formulář (JRF), which registers her simultaneously with the tax office, ČSSZ (social insurance) and her health insurer. Fee **1,000 Kč** for first registration.
- She receives an **IČO**. From that point, IČO, name and address must appear on invoices and on the linked website/order page (§435 občanského zákoníku).

## 2. Income tax — three possible regimes

| Regime | How it works | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Paušální daň (flat tax)** | One monthly payment covers income tax + social + health. Band 1 ≈ **8,700 Kč/month [verify 2026 figure]**. No tax return, minimal records. Must opt in by **10 January** of the year (or at registration). | Only if she is **not** an employee and **not** on parental allowance — otherwise it usually costs more than it saves. |
| **Flat expense rate (paušální výdaje)** | Declare income; deduct **60%** as deemed expenses for a free trade (cap 1,200,000 Kč income) **[verify]**. Tax 15% on the remainder after the personal allowance (30,840 Kč). Keep only income records. | The default for a small maker. Materials are cheap relative to labour, so 60% deemed expenses beats actual expenses. |
| **Actual expenses** | Full income/expense records. | Rarely worth it at this scale. |

**Parental leave caveat.** The audit mentions a 2-year-old. If Anna receives *rodičovský příspěvek*, running a trade is permitted and the allowance is not reduced **[verify]**. Her business counts as *vedlejší činnost* (secondary activity), which means:
- **No** minimum social-insurance advances in the first year; social contributions only if annual profit exceeds ~**110,000 Kč [verify yearly threshold]**.
- **No** minimum health-insurance advances while the state is paying her health insurance as a parent.
She must tick "vedlejší činnost" on the ČSSZ registration and provide the reason. Same applies if she is also employed.

**Filing.** Tax return by 1 April (2 May if filed electronically) **[verify]**; social and health overviews within a month after that.

## 3. VAT — irrelevant now, one hidden trap later

- Registration threshold: **2,000,000 Kč** turnover per calendar year **[verify]**. Not a concern.
- **The trap: "identifikovaná osoba" (identified person, §6h zákona o DPH).** If a Czech non-VAT trader *buys a service from a business in another country* — Meta advertising (Ireland), Canva Pro (Australia), Linktree Pro, Shopify, Etsy fees — she must register as an identified person **within 15 days**, declare and pay Czech VAT on that service (reverse charge, 21%), and file a VAT return for that month. It does not make her a full VAT payer and does not add VAT to her sales, but failing to register is a fineable omission that catches many small sellers. This is one more reason the "no paid Meta ads" decision is convenient. **Apify is a Czech company** (Apify Technologies s.r.o., Prague), so Apify subscriptions do not trigger this.
- Selling **goods** to consumers in other EU states: below **€10,000/year** cross-border total, Czech rules apply and nothing changes. Above that, the EU OSS scheme **[verify]**.
- Shipping outside the EU (UK, US, Switzerland): recipient may pay import duty/VAT; state this in terms.

## 4. Consumer law — what applies to made-to-order goods

Source: občanský zákoník (č. 89/2012 Sb.) and zákon o ochraně spotřebitele (č. 634/1992 Sb.). A DM order is a **distance contract** and consumer rules apply in full.

| Point | Rule | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Right of withdrawal | 14-day withdrawal (§1829 OZ) **does not apply** to goods made to the consumer's specification or personalised (§1837 písm. d)). It **does apply** to the ready-made "skladem / in stock" pieces sold remotely. | State clearly *before* confirming an order that custom pieces cannot be returned; accept 14-day returns on stock pieces. |
| Pre-contract information | Seller identity, IČO, address, total price, delivery cost, lead time, complaint procedure, note on withdrawal (§1820 OZ). | Put these on the linked page as *Obchodní podmínky / Terms*. A DM confirming "price X, delivery Y, custom = no return" satisfies the contract-level duty. |
| Price display | Prices offered to consumers must be final (incl. any VAT — none here — and stating delivery separately) (§13 zák. 634/1992) **[verify section]**. | "od 890 Kč" in the bio is acceptable as an indicative price; the confirmed DM price must be final. |
| Defects / warranty | Consumer may claim defects appearing within **2 years**; complaints must be resolved within **30 days** (§2165, §2172–2173 OZ) **[verify post-2023 numbering]**. | Handmade does not exempt her. Photograph every piece before handover. |
| Alternative dispute resolution | Must inform consumers that the Czech Trade Inspection (ČOI) is the ADR body (§14 zák. 634/1992). | One line in the terms. |
| Deposits | A deposit (záloha) for custom work is lawful and advisable (30–50%). | State it in the order steps. |

## 5. Data protection (GDPR) and personality rights

- Client names, addresses, phone numbers, **children's names and birth dates**, wedding dates — all personal data. Keep a two-line processing record (what, why, how long); publish a short privacy notice on the linked page; keep order data only as long as tax law requires (invoices: keep records for the tax audit period, in practice 10 years is the safe default).
- **Reposting client photos**, especially of children, needs **explicit consent** (§84–85 OZ personality rights + GDPR). A screenshot of the client saying "yes, you can share" in DM is sufficient; save it with the order.
- **Prospecting** (file 04): collecting public *business* profiles is a legitimate interest. Collecting profiles of private individuals for marketing is not. Keep the pipeline strictly B2B.
- **Unsolicited messages** (zákon č. 480/2004 Sb., §7): marketing emails/messages need prior consent. Individually written partnership proposals to businesses are low-risk; bulk or templated blasts are not. Include the sender's identity and an easy opt-out line in emails.

## 6. Intellectual property — a quiet risk in the current feed

- **Licensed characters** (Disney, Paw Patrol, Peppa, Mickey, football club crests) may not be embroidered for sale. Client requests for these must be declined; original bears, rabbits, aeroplanes are fine.
- **Pet portraits from client photos:** ask the client to confirm they own the photo (a professional photographer's image needs the photographer's permission).
- **Music in reels:** 15 of 19 reels use commercial tracks. On a Business account Meta restricts the commercial music library; reels may be muted or blocked in some regions. Switch to original audio or the royalty-free library.

## 7. Records and payments — minimum setup

- **EET** (electronic sales registration) was abolished in 2023; no cash-register obligation.
- Open a **separate bank account** for the trade (not legally required for OSVČ, but it makes the income record trivial). Accept bank transfer / QR payment; for foreign clients, Wise or PayPal — note PayPal fees are a foreign service (see §3 identified-person trap only if she pays for a *subscription*; transaction fees on payments received are generally not a purchased service **[verify with accountant]**).
- Issue a simple **faktura / receipt** for every order: her name, address, IČO, "Neplátce DPH", client name, description, price, date. A free tool (Fakturoid, iDoklad) does this in a minute.
- Keep a spreadsheet: date · client · piece · price · deposit · paid · shipped · consent for photos (Y/N).

## 8. Order of operations

1. Accountant consultation (30–60 min) → confirm regime and parental-leave status.
2. Register volná živnost via JRF → obtain IČO.
3. Register as OSVČ vedlejší činnost with ČSSZ and health insurer (JRF does this).
4. Decide flat expense rate vs. paušální daň.
5. Write *Obchodní podmínky / Terms* + privacy notice on the linked page; add IČO.
6. Set up invoicing tool and separate account.
7. Only then: switch the Instagram profile to Business and publish the new bio with prices.
