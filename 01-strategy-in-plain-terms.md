# 01 · The Plan in Plain Terms

Read this file first. It explains the whole approach without jargon. The other files are the working documents.

---

## The one-sentence diagnosis

Anna is broadcasting to the wrong crowd: 86 of her 92 followers are friends or other embroiderers, and only 6 are Czech people who could actually buy a piece and pick it up in Prague.

So the job is not "get more followers". The job is **get the right 500 people to see the page, and make it easy for them to order.**

---

## Who actually buys this

Not the baby. Not the couple. The **gift-giver**: a grandmother, a godparent, a bridesmaid, a friend. They have a deadline (a birth, a wedding, a birthday) and a worry: *"I want to bring something nobody else will bring."*

Everything on the page should speak to that person.

---

## Three things, in order

### 1. Fix the shop window (week 1)

Right now, a stranger landing on the page sees pretty embroidery and no way to know what it costs, how to order, or how long it takes. That's a closed shop with a nice display.

Fix: switch to a Business profile, rewrite the bio, add a link, pin three posts (how to order + prices, reviews, best reel), refresh the highlights.

→ See `02-page-setup-checklist.md` and `03-bio-and-pinned-posts-copy.md`.

### 2. Be present every day without selling every day (ongoing)

You are right that daily presence ≠ daily new product. The rule:

- **Feed: 3–4 posts a week.** Each finished piece is shown from several angles across a week (process → reveal → on the wall → client reaction). Never the same video twice with the same caption.
- **Stories: every day.** Stories are the daily presence. They disappear in 24 hours, so they can be small and unpolished.

Non-selling angles that build familiarity (rotate these):

| Angle | Example |
|---|---|
| Behind the scenes | Thread colour choice, the messy desk, morning coffee before stitching |
| Time-lapse / satisfying | 8 seconds of one flower being finished |
| "Ask me" | Story poll: "Bear or bunny for this nursery?" — followers vote, feel involved |
| Client story (with consent) | "Karl the poodle's owner sent me this photo" |
| Education | "Why I use linen, not cotton" · "How to hang a hoop without a nail" |
| Occasion calendar | "Wedding season starts in May — ring trays take 3 weeks, so order by mid-April" |
| Personal | Her 2-year-old son helping (the birthday post got 1,042 views — the highest personal reach) |
| Prague | Where she buys thread, a Prague café, the tram — this plants the *local* signal Instagram uses to show the page to Prague users |

### 3. Find the buyers where they gather (weeks 2–8)

Here is the part you asked me to re-explain.

**What I advised against:** building a list of pregnant women and engaged couples and messaging them one by one. Reasons: Instagram will block the account, EU privacy law forbids collecting individuals' data for marketing without a legal basis, and cold messages to strangers about a gift don't convert anyway.

**What I advise instead:** build a list of **businesses** that meet the gift-givers before you do — and turn them into referrers.

Think of it like this. A newborn photographer in Prague photographs 15 babies a month. Each of those families has grandparents and friends looking for a gift *right now*. If the photographer uses Anna's hoop as a prop and tags her, Anna is shown to all 15 families and their gift-givers, with the photographer's implicit endorsement. One partner = a stream of warm buyers, every month, for free.

**So what do you do with the list?** The list is a list of *partners to approach*, not customers to sell to. For each one:

1. Follow them, like and comment on a few posts over a week (so the name is familiar).
2. Send **one** personal message or email (written by hand, 5 per day max) offering a free sample piece in exchange for using it as a prop / displaying it / tagging her.
3. Send the sample. Photograph the handover. Post about the collaboration.
4. Track replies in a spreadsheet. Follow up once after 10 days. Then stop.

50 partner targets → realistically 8–12 replies → 4–6 active partnerships. That is enough to change the account's audience within two months.

Apify is fine for **collecting the business profiles** (public business accounts, not private individuals). Not for sending anything.

→ See `04-partner-prospecting-pipeline.md` for the target types, the scoring, the Apify workflow and the message templates.

## Also: the customers she already has

~20 pieces have been delivered. Zero customers have ever tagged the page. Each of those hoops is on a nursery wall being photographed weekly.

Fix: every piece now leaves with a small card (handle, QR code, "tag me when it's on the wall", and a discount code for the buyer's *next* occasion). Message the past 20 clients once, personally, asking for a photo and a tag. This costs nothing and is the fastest source of both content and social proof.

## Czech legal and tax

Selling regularly on Instagram without a trade licence is illegal in Czechia, and personalised goods have specific consumer-law rules. Nothing dramatic, but it must be set up before the page starts pulling real orders.

→ See `05-czech-tax-and-legal-caveats.md`.

## What "success" looks like in 8 weeks

| Metric | Now | Target (8 weeks) |
|---|---|---|
| Czech / Prague-based followers | 6 | 150+ |
| Tagged posts by customers | 0 | 10+ |
| Active partner businesses | 0 | 4–6 |
| Story posted per week | 0 | 6–7 |
| Enquiries via DM/link per week | unknown | tracked, ≥5 |
| Distinct posts / re-posts ratio | 20 / 35 | no duplicate captions |

Followers are the last number to move. Enquiries and tags come first.
